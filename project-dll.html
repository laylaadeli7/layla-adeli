Low-power, low-jitter 8 GHz delay-locked loop

EE 124, Mixed-Mode Integrated Circuits · 45 nm CMOS · Cadence Virtuoso, Spectre, ADE/Maestro · 2026
45 nm CMOSDLLcharge pump VCDLjitterCadence

A complete DLL — phase-frequency detector, charge pump, loop filter, and voltage-controlled delay line — with every device sized by hand for lock range, static phase offset, and jitter. Awarded Best DLL Project in EE 124 for class-best power, static phase offset, and jitter.

Architecture

A DLL locks the delay through a chain to exactly one clock period, which gives you precisely spaced output phases — useful for clock distribution and high-speed links, and unlike a PLL it can't accumulate phase error because there's no oscillator in the loop. This design produces four phases (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) at 8 GHz.

Phase-frequency detector. A pass-transistor DFF-based PFD compares the reference clock against the delay-line output and emits UP/DN pulses. A reset path resolves the dead zone, which is what keeps static phase offset low near lock.

Charge pump and loop filter. The charge pump converts UP/DN pulses into a control voltage Vctrl. Pump current and filter capacitance were sized together for lock within 5 ns with enough phase margin not to ring, and the mirrored biasing keeps charge and discharge paths symmetric so the loop doesn't settle to a false offset.

Voltage-controlled delay line. Eight cascaded current-starved inverter cells, delay tuned by Vctrl. A non-starved output buffer restores a near-square wave, and mirrored starvation biasing keeps duty-cycle distortion under 1%.

Results

The loop locks within 5 ns for all four required input phases, under both clean and noisy supply and input conditions. Maximum static phase offset is 1.36% of a clock period, and the phase transfer characteristic is linear across the full 2π range.
