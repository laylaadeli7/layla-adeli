A 192-electrode neural recording front end with continuous on-chip channel selection

Senior thesis · MICS Lab, Caltech · 65 nm CMOS · Advisor: Prof. Azita Emami
65 nm CMOSLNASAR ADC Gm-Con-chip learningneural interfaces

A recording front end that records only the 32 most informative of 192 electrodes, and re-selects them as the signals drift, using unsupervised statistics computed on chip.

Why channel selection

High-density arrays are necessary for brain–computer interfaces to work well, but power scales with channel count, and an implant's power budget is set by how much it can heat the tissue around it. The way out is that most channels are not carrying much: activity is rare, redundant across neighbors, and the useful subset changes over days as the array settles and the subject moves.

So rather than digitizing everything, the chip picks the N best channels out of M total, keeps those on a full recording path, and surveys the rest cheaply in the background — re-ranking as the signals change.

The selection rule

Electrodes are scored by how much their spike-band activity varies over time, which needs no labels and no training, so it can run continuously on chip. To check that this is a reasonable proxy for information content, I built a simulation framework on public motor-cortex recordings and compared selection rules against decoding accuracy.

Across 14 sessions spanning 3.5 years, the top 32 of 96 channels chosen this way keep 96% of all-channel decoding accuracy. Random selection of the same number of channels keeps 50%; a supervised oracle that is allowed to see labels reaches 97%.

The same framework sets the survey hardware's specifications — resolution, bin length, and survey duration — by sweeping each one against decoding accuracy, so the analog design targets come from what the decoder actually needs rather than from a guess.

Front-end design

ParameterTarget
Process65 nm CMOS
Array size192 electrodes, 32 recorded concurrently
Sample rate10 kS/s per channel
Resolution≥ 9 ENOB with the LNA attached (prior design: 8.1)
Power per channel< 1 µW
Total power target≈ 30 µW including survey and selection logic
Signal pathLNA → Gm-C integrating sampler → 4 shared 10-bit asynchronous SAR ADCs

Four asynchronous SAR ADCs are shared across the 32 active channels rather than one per channel, which is where much of the area and power saving comes from. The Gm-C integrating sampler in front of each converter does the anti-alias filtering and holds the sample, so the LNA never has to drive the comparator directly.

Status

The selection rule and the simulation framework are done and validated on public data. The recording path and the survey path are in design; the next steps are closing the ENOB target with the LNA in the loop and laying out the selection logic that ranks all 192 electrodes.
